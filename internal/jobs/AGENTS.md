# jobs

Local process supervision for session-owned jobs; no SDK dependencies.
- `Process` owns its direct child and drains combined stdout and stderr to the caller's writer. The writer must return and must not call back into the process. Unclosed inherited output pipes report `ErrOutputIncomplete`.
- `Spec.Stdin` opens a writable pipe; otherwise stdin receives EOF. `CloseStdin`, `Kill`, and `Close` close it and unblock concurrent writes. Do not hold the lifecycle mutex across a write. The first `Close` reports recorded errors; later calls return nil without work.
- Unix jobs use dedicated process groups. Escalate before reaping: the zombie leader pins the PGID. After reaping, `Kill` and `Signal` refuse to touch the PGID. Descendants that leave the group are outside this guarantee.
- Linux owns the group through the pidfd created with the leader (`SysProcAttr.PidFD`, not `pidfd_open` afterwards): signals use `PIDFD_SIGNAL_PROCESS_GROUP` and the disappearance probe resolves that same identity, so a recycled numeric PGID is never reached. The capability is probed before any user command runs (signal 0 on our own descriptor, no delivery), and an unsupported kernel refuses startup with `ErrProcessIdentityUnsupported`. There is no numeric PID or PGID fallback anywhere: an unverified identity refuses, and a launch whose identity cannot be verified reclaims only the direct child through its kernel handle, reporting the group as unresolved. Darwin and Windows keep numeric group handling and their weaker guarantee.
- Exit observation only reports: Linux polls the same pidfd and accepts `POLLIN` alone as exit evidence (`POLLNVAL`/`POLLERR` are reported, not read as an exit), Darwin uses kqueue plus a zombie check, Windows the process handle. Other platforms reject startup with `ErrWatcherUnsupported`; never pretend safe escalation is available there. A passive identity observation holds no lifecycle lock: Linux duplicates the pidfd under the owner mutex (`F_DUPFD_CLOEXEC`) and polls that private copy, so a released descriptor or a reused descriptor number can neither end nor redirect a pending wait, and the copy is closed when the observation ends.
- A failed observation never loses ownership. `Signal`, `Kill` and `Close` still act through the group identity and the original diagnostic is reported next to the recovered result. Cleanup first establishes termination from the identity, bounded by a deadline, so a survivor cannot hold the caller and no reaper runs for a live child; a leader reaped elsewhere has no status: cleanup still runs, `ErrExitUnavailable` is recorded, and no `Exit` is invented. Cleanup without a real leader status keeps `Running=true`, retains the slot and reports `ErrJobCleanupIncomplete`.
- One gate serializes signalling, escalation and our own reap. `Signal` holds it across its refusal check and its delivery, so nothing reaches the group after our reap and an admitted escalation finishes signalling first. `Kill` holds it from admission through the reap that publishes the status, and `Wait` observes passively without it. A reap runs under the gate only after exit evidence, and it publishes the leader status there. Once evidence exists, `Wait` admits at most one background collector and answers its context while that collector waits for the gate, so a pending wait never stands between cleanup and its reap; output draining runs after the gate is released. A completed reap is reported from its record, so `Wait` after `Close` returns the real result instead of probing a released identity. The identity descriptor stays open until the leader is reaped and the exit watcher returned; `Close` closes it exactly once, and unproven cleanup keeps it.
- Windows group termination is unsupported and reports `ErrGroupKillUnsupported` after terminating the direct child.
- `LogStore` owns raw output bytes. Readers use immutable snapshots; they never hold the writer lock. Pages are at most 64 KiB, and cursor gaps identify the first retained byte.
- Session quota evicts whole logs FIFO by the start of the current retention window, breaking ties by job ID; the appending job is protected and tail-capped to the smaller quota. `Facts` reports evicted logs. Appending after eviction or `Drop` resumes at the old offset with any loss recorded.
- Dropped logs keep offset metadata so a later `Append` resumes at the old offset; `Forget` removes bytes and metadata permanently. Only `Forget` bounds that metadata.
- `Manager` owns background admission, processes and logs. Request contexts end at admission; `MaxActive` bounds in-flight and active jobs.
- `MaxFinished` (default 20) bounds retained terminal states: each completion retires the oldest terminal states beyond the cap and forgets their log metadata once no producer remains. Running and unresolved jobs are never retired. `Retired` counts the dropped states for the list result.
- Job IDs are `<16-hex manager prefix><16-hex sequence>`; sequences only grow, so IDs are never reused, and an issued-but-retired ID reports `ErrJobRetired` (which also matches `ErrJobNotFound`) instead of being confused with a foreign ID. The prefix carries the unguessability, so never shrink it to a counter alone.
- `Wait` that attached to a job answers that job's own terminal state, so retirement cannot turn it into a lookup failure. `Status`, `Wait`, `Cancel` and `Output` on a retired ID report `ErrJobRetired`, and its log answers `ErrLogNotFound`.
- On natural leader exit, kill the remaining group before reaping; `JobState.Cancelled` records whether cancellation or shutdown won before observed exit. Windows reports group termination unsupported even on natural exit.
- Group disappearance is checked once `Kill` finishes and reported as `ErrGroupNotGone`.
- `control.go` forwards manager stdin, EOF and signals through retained job ownership. Host adapters own foreground waits/promotion and tool contracts; this package does not provide the retention UI.
